**812 patients, 50 pathways, 5 independent study models.** The primary analysis found **0 of 250 pathway–study interactions at FDR < 0.05** (smallest age-interaction q = 0.986). The separate any-TB-difference test identified 6 pathway–study associations at FDR < 0.05.

The data do **not currently provide FDR-controlled evidence that the TB–nonTB enrichment difference changes with age** under this model. The curves are exploratory estimates with uncertainty; visible bends should not be called established age dynamics.

The supplementary fixed-bin analysis found **7 of 550 tested study–pathway–bin comparisons at FDR < 0.05** (minimum q = 0.024). These are within-age-range associations under separate linear-age models. Their significance cannot be assigned to every exact age in the bin or used to claim an age-dependent TB difference. The original exact-age analysis and all continuous curves remain unchanged.

Combining studies within the same bins, with study and clinical adjustment, found **16 of 100 pooled comparisons at FDR < 0.05** (minimum q = 0.0013). The separate TB-by-study diagnostic identified 0 of 100 FDR-significant heterogeneity tests; that does not validate the common-effect assumption. Study-omission estimates and unavailable models remain visible. Pooling is exploratory and cannot create evidence from a single-study childhood bin or overcome an unstable adjusted fit.

No model identifies a continuous childhood-to-old-age trajectory. Children and adults were recruited in different studies, adolescence is unsampled, and sparse coverage and model stability limit the oldest-age comparisons. More age-overlapping cohorts and longitudinal sampling would distinguish age from study effects.
